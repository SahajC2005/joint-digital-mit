Self-supervised knot placement · Chhetri, 2026

One Transformer learns where every knot should go.

A cubic B-spline always passes exactly through its data points — the only freedom left is a handful of numbers between 0 and 1 called interior knots, and they alone decide whether the curve glides or whiplashes. This page takes apart, piece by piece, how a single encoder–decoder Transformer learns to place those knots for any number of points: how points become tokens, how learned queries interrogate the geometry, and how a curvature energy trains the whole thing with no labels at all.

u = 0.00 · the problem

A curve with exactly K = N−2 free dials

Given N ordered points, a clamped cubic B-spline is built from a knot vector — a non-decreasing list of numbers that tells the curve how to spend its parameter time between the points:

\[ U = \{\underbrace{0,0,0,0}_{\text{clamp}},\; u_1, u_2, \dots, u_K,\; \underbrace{1,1,1,1}_{\text{clamp}}\}, \qquad C(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n} N_{i,3}(t)\,P_i \]
Eq. (1) — the curve is a blend of control points Pᵢ weighted by Cox–de Boor basis functions Nᵢ,₃(t), whose shapes are carved entirely by the knots.
fixed

N + 6 knots

Eight of them are pinned at 0 and 1 (multiplicity 4) so the curve starts at the first point and ends at the last.

determined

N + 2 control points

Once knots are chosen, the control points are the unique solution of a linear system. No choice left here.

free

K = N − 2 interior knots

The only design variables in the whole construction. These K numbers in (0, 1) are what the Transformer must predict.

The control points come from the interpolation conditions plus natural end conditions \(C''(0)=C''(1)=0\), giving a square \((N{+}2)\times(N{+}2)\) system:

\[ A(u)\,P = \tilde{D} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad P(u) = A(u)^{-1}\tilde{D} \]
Eq. (6) — A(u) is the collocation matrix of basis values at the data parameters; it depends on the knots u, which is exactly how the knots reach the curve.
Remark 1 · “Fit is free”

For any valid knot vector the solution exists and the curve passes exactly through every data point. There is no fit-vs-smoothness trade-off here — we are choosing among a family of perfectly interpolating curves, hunting for the smoothest one. That's why the lab above shows two curves through identical points.

u = 0.14 · why knots matter

Knot spacing lives in a denominator

Differentiate a B-spline and the knots fall straight into a denominator. The derivative's control points are scaled first differences:

\[ C'(t)=\sum_i N_{i,2}(t)\,Q_i, \qquad Q_i = \frac{p}{\,u_{i+p+1}-u_{i+1}\,}\,\bigl(P_{i+1}-P_i\bigr) \]
Eq. (7) — squeeze two knots together and uᵢ₊₄ − uᵢ₊₁ shrinks, so Qᵢ explodes: the curve is forced to sprint through that region.

Curvature compounds the effect — it divides by speed cubed, so wherever the curve slows to a crawl, κ can blow up toward a cusp:

\[ \kappa(t) = \frac{x'y'' - y'x''}{\left(x'^2+y'^2\right)^{3/2}}, \qquad E_{\mathrm{curv}}(u)=\int_0^1 \kappa(t)^2\,\lVert C'(t)\rVert\,dt \]
Eqs. (9)–(10) — total bending energy: the single number the entire system is trained to minimize. Approximated with M = 100 quadrature samples.

u = 0.29 · the full forward pass

Points in, knots out, gradients all the way back

Here is the whole machine (Figure 1 of the paper). Everything from raw coordinates to scalar energy is differentiable — including the linear solve — so the curvature of the final curve can teach the very first attention layer.

The dashed raspberry path is the gradient. Training never sees a “correct” knot vector — the loss is the bending energy of the curve the knots produce. Each block below gets its own section.

u = 0.43 · stage one · encoder

Every point becomes a token that can see every other point

The encoder's job is to turn raw coordinates into a geometric description rich enough for knot placement. Each data point Dᵏ is first dressed up with local context into a 6-feature token:

token = 6 numbers per point
featurewhat it tells the model
x, ynormalized position of the point itself
d₋, d₊distances to the previous and next neighbor — the raw material of the chordal heuristic
θturning angle at this point — the thing chordal ignores, and the best predictor of where curvature will concentrate
i/Nnormalized sequence position
then: lift & mix

A linear layer projects each 6-vector to d_model = 64, a learned positional embedding is added, and 3 encoder layers of multi-head self-attention (h = 4 heads, d_k = 16) let every token aggregate information from every other token:

\[ \mathrm{Attn}(Q,K,V)=\mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V \]

Self-attention is what makes global quantities computable: a chord fraction needs the total length of the polyline, which no single point knows alone.

● Self-attention — tap a token
Weights shown are an illustrative pattern (real heads are learned and there are 4 × 3 of them), but the structure is faithful: a token attends across the whole sequence, near and far, and padded slots are forced to weight 0 by adding −∞ inside the softmax. That mask is half of the variable-length trick: one model, any N ≤ 10.
u = 0.57 · stage two · decoder queries

Seven learned questions, asked of the geometry

This is the heart of the architecture, borrowed from DETR. The decoder owns K max + 1 = 7 learnable query vectors — think of them as seven standing questions baked into the weights: “where should the 1st knot go?”, “where should the 2nd knot go?”, … plus one special slack slot.

Each of the 3 decoder layers does two things, in order:

1 · self-attention among queries

The seven queries talk to each other. Knots must be ordered and well spaced, so query 3 needs to know what queries 2 and 4 are planning. This is where the knot vector is coordinated as a set rather than predicted independently.

2 · cross-attention to the points

Each query then attends over the encoder's memory — the N point tokens — and pulls out exactly the geometric evidence it needs. Q comes from the query, K and V come from the points: the question interrogates the data.

● Cross-attention — select a query, see what it reads

Seven points (N = 7) means K = N − 2 = 5 knots, so only queries q₁…q₅ + slack are switched on by the per-sample num_knots selector — q₆ simply sits idle. That selection is the other half of variable-length output: the same 7 queries serve every sequence length, which is precisely why a model trained on N ∈ {4, 5} can answer for N = 8 using query slots it never exercised in training. Attention ribbons are illustrative; each query plausibly concentrates on the stretch of curve its knot will govern.
the slack slot

Why 7 queries for at most 6 knots? The output head (next section) normalizes a running sum, and without a spare slot the last knot gets mathematically pinned near 1.0 — an earlier version without slack degenerated. The slack query absorbs the leftover interval so every real knot lands strictly inside (0, 1).

u = 0.71 · stage three · monotonic head

How seven raw numbers become a legal knot vector

A knot vector must be strictly increasing inside (0, 1) — a hard constraint a neural net can't be trusted to respect by accident. The head builds the constraint into the arithmetic, so any raw output is a valid knot vector:

\[ s_i = \mathrm{softplus}(r_i) + \varepsilon \;>\,0, \qquad c_i=\sum_{j\le i} s_j \;\uparrow, \qquad u_i = \frac{c_i}{c_{K+1}} \in (0,1) \]
Eq. (14) — softplus makes every step positive, the cumulative sum makes the sequence increasing, and dividing by the total (which includes the slack step) squeezes everything strictly below 1. Order is guaranteed by construction, not learned.
● The head, in your hands — set the raw scalars rᵢ
Set the sliders to anything — even wildly out of order — and the resulting knots are always sorted and inside (0, 1). The gold band is the slack step: it never becomes a knot, it just keeps u₄ off the wall. At evaluation the knots are additionally compressed into [0.02, 0.98] so the linear solver never sees a degenerate knot vector.
u = 0.86 · stage four · the energy is the teacher

No labels, anywhere: the curve grades itself

Nobody knows the “correct” knots — computing them per sample is exactly the expensive optimization we're trying to amortize away. So training happens in two phases, and in the second one the loss is the physics:

phase 1 · chordal pretraining
\[ \mathcal{L}_1(\theta)=\tfrac{1}{|B|}\textstyle\sum_{i\in B} \lVert f_\theta(D_i) - u_i^{\mathrm{chord}}\rVert_1 \]

100 epochs of supervised imitation of the chordal heuristic (Adam, η = 10⁻³, batch 64). Not because chordal is good — because it's a sane starting point on the energy landscape instead of a random cliff edge.

phase 2 · energy fine-tuning
\[ \mathcal{L}_2(\theta)=\tfrac{1}{|B|}\textstyle\sum_{i\in B} \min\!\bigl(E_{\mathrm{curv}}(f_\theta(D_i)),\,E_{\mathrm{cap}}\bigr) \]

300 epochs of pure self-supervision (Adam, η = 10⁻⁵, batch 128): predict knots → build the curve → measure its bending energy → backpropagate. E_cap = 1000 stops cusp outliers (energies up to 10⁸!) from hijacking the gradient.

The gradient has to go through a matrix inverse

The chain rule splits the gradient into a Transformer part and a geometry part — and the geometry part includes the linear solve. Differentiating an inverse uses the implicit-function identity:

\[ \frac{\partial E}{\partial \theta} \;=\; \underbrace{\frac{\partial E}{\partial u}}_{\text{energy vs. knots}}\cdot \underbrace{\frac{\partial u}{\partial \theta}}_{\text{knots vs. weights}}, \qquad\quad \frac{\partial P}{\partial u_k} = -A^{-1}\,\frac{\partial A}{\partial u_k}\,P \]
Eqs. (17)–(18) — “how would the control points shift if knot k nudged?” is answered by re-using the already-factored system. Autograd handles it; the point is that nothing in the chain blocks the gradient.
batching trick · N-grouped solver

A batch mixes lengths (N = 4 and N = 5 give different matrix sizes). Samples are grouped by N inside each mini-batch so each group becomes one batched torch.linalg.solve call — a partial answer to the “batched variable-length energy layer” the original thesis flagged as future work. A fully jagged implementation is still open.

Training data: 4,000 random sequences in [−0.5, 0.5]², filtered to chordal κ < 1000 → 3,187 samples, all at N ∈ {4, 5}. Lengths 6, 7, 8 are never seen.

Why does it generalize? The encoder rediscovers geometry

Linear probes (ridge regression) on the encoder's internal representations show it has learned to compute the classical quantities from raw coordinates — the very features the original per-length MLPs had to be hand-fed:

Linear-probe R² on encoder representations. Chord and centripetal fractions — global quantities requiring the whole sequence — are near-perfectly decodable, which is only possible because self-attention mixes information across all tokens.

What's still open

The head pins knot spacing through a cumulative sum (an autoregressive or beta-distributed head is future work); experiments are 2-D and on random uniform points, not CAD outlines or handwriting; the only baseline is chordal; and a single forward pass carries no convergence guarantee — a hybrid “Transformer init + a few gradient steps” would close the remaining amortization gap.
