Three different fates, one guarantee covering them all. The mathematics we are about to build says every walker provably stops at a critical point — a flat spot. The deep valley is a flat spot. The trap valley is a flat spot. The shelf is so nearly flat the walker may never visibly settle. Our research question was never whether PALM converges (that is a 2014 theorem). It was: on sparse tensor-train problems, which of these three fates actually occurs, when, and why?

To even state that question properly, we need the cube, the train, the tax, and the walker — each built carefully. Begin the ascent.

Camp 01 · objects

A cube of numbers

A list of numbers is a 1-D array. A spreadsheet is a 2-D array (a matrix). Stack spreadsheets and you get a 3-D array — a tensor. Formally, an order-\(d\) tensor is a map \(\mathcal T \in \mathbb R^{n_1\times n_2\times\cdots\times n_d}\): one real number \(\mathcal T_{i_1 i_2 \cdots i_d}\) for every choice of \(d\) indices. Our experiments used \(d=3\) with \(n_1=n_2=n_3=6\), so \(6^3 = 216\) numbers, and a check at \(10^3=1000\).

Slide through the cube below, layer by layer. Each square is one number; darker means larger magnitude, red means negative. Notice something important for later: most entries are exactly zero (blank). That is the kind of ground truth we manufactured.

Experiment 1 — slice explorera 6×6×6 sparse tensor
nonzeros in this slice nonzeros in the whole cube (of 216)

Why care about tensors at all? Because real data is naturally multi-indexed: a video is (height × width × time), a chemistry simulation is (particle × particle × particle × …), a recommendation dataset is (user × item × context). And the curse is immediate: sizes multiply. A modest \(100^4\) tensor already holds \(10^8\) numbers. Storing the raw cube does not scale — which is the cue for compression.

Camp 02 · structure

The tensor train

The tensor-train (TT) decomposition (Oseledets, 2011) refuses to store the cube. It stores \(d\) small 3-way blocks called cores,

\(G_k \in \mathbb R^{\,r_{k-1}\times n_k \times r_k}, \qquad k = 1,\dots,d, \qquad r_0 = r_d = 1,\)

and declares that the cube's entries are reconstructed by a chain of matrix products. Fix the indices \(i_1,\dots,i_d\). Then \(G_k[:,i_k,:]\) is an \(r_{k-1}\times r_k\) matrix (a slice of core \(k\)), and

\[\mathcal T_{i_1 i_2 \cdots i_d} \;=\; G_1[:,i_1,:]\; G_2[:,i_2,:]\;\cdots\; G_d[:,i_d,:].\]

The left and right ends have rank-1 bonds, so this product of matrices collapses to a single number — exactly the entry we wanted. The numbers \(r_1,\dots,r_{d-1}\) are the TT ranks: the width of the pipe carrying information between neighboring cores. Storage drops from \(\prod_k n_k\) (exponential in \(d\)) to \(\sum_k r_{k-1} n_k r_k\) (linear in \(d\)). That is the entire economic case for tensor trains — feel it with the sliders:

Experiment 2 — the compression ledgerstorage: cube vs train

Two facts to bank for later. First, the map from cores to cube is multilinear: linear in each core separately, but a product across cores — and products of unknowns are exactly what make optimization landscapes rugged. Second, raising the rank \(r\) buys expressive power but, as our survey will show, it is also one of the two knobs that summon trouble.

Camp 03 · the regularizer

The sparsity tax

We want the recovered cores themselves to be sparse — mostly exact zeros — for interpretability and extra compression. The honest way to ask for sparsity is to count nonzeros, the so-called \(\ell_0\) "norm", but counting is combinatorial and brutal to optimize. The classical surrogate is the \(\ell_1\) norm, the sum of absolute values:

\[\|G\|_1 = \sum_{\text{entries}} |G_{\text{entry}}|.\]

We charge each core a tax of \(\lambda\,\|G_k\|_1\). The magic of \(\ell_1\) is that, inside the right algorithm, it produces exact zeros — via an operation called soft-thresholding, which we will derive as a theorem at Camp 05. Preview it here: a vector goes in, every entry shrinks toward zero by \(\tau\lambda\), and anything that can't survive the shrink dies at exactly zero.

Experiment 3 — the thresholding guillotinesoft-threshold \(S_{\tau\lambda}\)
0 entries snapped to exact zero survivors shrink by exactly 0.35

Note the side effect you can see in the bars: survivors don't keep their full height — they are biased toward zero by the tax. This is why, at the end of our pipeline, we run a debiasing step: freeze which entries survived, switch the tax off, and re-fit only the survivors. In our study that one cheap polish improved recovery error by a median of 31%.

Camp 04 · the problem, stated

The objective function

We can now write the single formula the entire project orbits. Given a data tensor \(\mathcal T\) (our manufactured sparse TT ground truth plus 1% noise), find cores minimizing

The problem sparse TT decomposition

\[ \min_{G_1,\dots,G_d}\; F(G) \;=\; \underbrace{\tfrac12\,\bigl\|\mathcal T-\mathrm{TT}(G_1,\dots,G_d)\bigr\|_F^2}_{f(G)\ \text{— smooth fit}} \;+\; \underbrace{\lambda \sum_{k=1}^d \|G_k\|_1}_{g(G)\ \text{— nonsmooth tax}} \]

where \(\|\cdot\|_F\) is the Frobenius norm (square every entry, sum, square-root) and \(\lambda>0\) sets the tax rate. We used \(\lambda = 0.1\times\) the root-mean-square entry of \(\mathcal T\).

Read the anatomy. The fit term \(f\) is smooth (infinitely differentiable) but nonconvex, because \(\mathrm{TT}(\cdot)\) multiplies unknowns together. The tax \(g\) is convex but nonsmooth — the absolute value has a kink at zero, and the kink is precisely what manufactures exact zeros. So \(F\) is a nonconvex + nonsmooth composite: the exact class of problem PALM was invented for.

One more structural gift that our implementation exploits heavily: \(f\) is quadratic in each core separately. Freeze every core but \(G_k\); then the reconstruction is a linear function of \(G_k\), so \(f\) restricted to that block is a parabola-shaped bowl. Nonconvexity lives only in the coupling between blocks. PALM's whole strategy is to ride that gift: solve easy one-block problems, cycle forever.

Camp 05 · first theorems

Walking downhill, with proofs

Time to build the walker's two moves as actual mathematics: the downhill nudge (gradient step, justified by the Descent Lemma) and the tax collection (proximal map, which for \(\ell_1\) is exactly the guillotine from Camp 03).

Move 1 — the gradient step and the Descent Lemma

The gradient \(\nabla f(x)\) points uphill; \(-\nabla f(x)\) points downhill. But how big a step is safe? The answer is governed by how fast the slope itself can change — the Lipschitz constant of the gradient: a number \(L\) with \(\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\| \le L\|x-y\|\) for all \(x,y\). Curvier functions have bigger \(L\).

Lemma Descent Lemma / quadratic majorization

If \(\nabla f\) is \(L\)-Lipschitz, then for all \(x, y\): \[ f(y) \;\le\; f(x) + \langle \nabla f(x),\, y-x\rangle + \tfrac{L}{2}\|y-x\|^2 .\]

Proof

Write \(f(y)-f(x)=\int_0^1 \langle \nabla f(x+t(y-x)),\,y-x\rangle\,dt\) (fundamental theorem of calculus along the segment). Add and subtract \(\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle\), bound the difference term by Cauchy–Schwarz and Lipschitzness: \(\langle \nabla f(x+t(y-x))-\nabla f(x),\,y-x\rangle \le L\,t\,\|y-x\|^2\). Integrate: \(\int_0^1 Lt\,dt = L/2\). ∎

Geometrically: above every point sits a parabola that majorizes \(f\) — it touches \(f\) at \(x\) and lies on or above \(f\) everywhere. Minimizing the parabola instead of \(f\) gives the step \(x^+ = x - \tfrac1L \nabla f(x)\), and because the parabola sits above \(f\), going to its bottom guarantees \(f\) decreases. Move 2 — the proximal map, and where the guillotine comes from

Definition proximal map

For a function \(g\) and step \(\tau>0\), \[ \operatorname{prox}_{\tau g}(v) \;=\; \arg\min_{u}\; \Bigl\{\, g(u) + \tfrac{1}{2\tau}\|u-v\|^2 \,\Bigr\}. \] In words: find the point that best balances "make \(g\) small" against "stay near \(v\)". It is the rigorous form of "take the gradient step, then pay the tax in the gentlest way possible."

Theorem prox of the \(\ell_1\) norm = soft-thresholding

For \(g(u)=\lambda\|u\|_1\), the prox splits entrywise, and for each scalar coordinate \[ \operatorname{prox}_{\tau\lambda|\cdot|}(v) \;=\; S_{\tau\lambda}(v) \;=\; \operatorname{sign}(v)\,\max(|v|-\tau\lambda,\,0). \]

Proof (scalar case)

Minimize \(h(u)=\lambda|u| + \frac{1}{2\tau}(u-v)^2\). Case \(u>0\): \(h'(u)=\lambda+\frac{u-v}{\tau}=0 \Rightarrow u = v-\tau\lambda\), valid only if \(v>\tau\lambda\). Case \(u<0\): symmetric, \(u=v+\tau\lambda\), valid if \(v<-\tau\lambda\). Otherwise no smooth stationary point exists with the right sign, and \(u=0\) wins: at zero the subdifferential of \(|u|\) is the whole interval \([-1,1]\), and the optimality condition \(0 \in \lambda[-1,1] + \frac{0-v}{\tau}\) holds exactly when \(|v|\le\tau\lambda\). Stitch the three cases: that's \(S_{\tau\lambda}\). ∎

This proof contains the first sighting of a crucial creature — the subdifferential, the set-valued generalization of "derivative" at kinks. It returns at Camp 07 to define what "the walker stopped" even means.

Camp 06 · the algorithm

PALM, precisely

Proximal Alternating Linearized Minimization (Bolte, Sabach & Teboulle, Mathematical Programming 146, 2014). One full sweep over the cores:

Pick block k, freeze the others

Restricted to \(G_k\), the fit \(f\) is an exact quadratic bowl (Camp 04's gift).

Compute the block gradient and its exact Lipschitz constant

\(\nabla_{G_k} f\) and \(L_k\) — see the boxed computation below; no guessing, no line search.

Gradient step with stepsize \(\tau_k = 1/(\gamma L_k)\), \(\gamma>1\)

We used \(\gamma = 1.1\). The margin \(\gamma>1\) is what powers the sufficient-decrease lemma.

Apply the prox of the tax: soft-threshold

\(G_k \leftarrow S_{\tau_k\lambda}\bigl(G_k - \tau_k \nabla_{G_k} f\bigr)\). Entries die at exact zero here.

Next block; repeat sweeps until steps vanish

Our convergence criterion: per-sweep movement below \(10^{-5}\max(1,\|\mathcal T\|_F)\).

Computation the exact block Lipschitz constant

Contract cores \(1..k\!-\!1\) into the left interface \(L_k\) and cores \(k\!+\!1..d\) into the right interface \(R_k\). Vectorizing, the reconstruction is linear in core \(k\): \(\operatorname{vec}(L_k\, G_k\, R_k) = (R_k^{\!\top}\!\otimes L_k)\operatorname{vec}(G_k)\), where \(\otimes\) is the Kronecker product. Hence \(\nabla_{G_k} f = M^{\!\top}(M\operatorname{vec}(G_k)-\operatorname{vec}(\mathcal T))\) with \(M=R_k^{\!\top}\!\otimes L_k\), whose gradient-Lipschitz constant is \(\|M^{\!\top} M\|_2 = \|M\|_2^2\). By the Kronecker spectral identity \(\|A\otimes B\|_2 = \|A\|_2\|B\|_2\), \[ L_k \;=\; \|L_k^{\!\top} L_k\|_2\,\cdot\,\|R_k R_k^{\!\top}\|_2 . \] This single formula is why our code never needs a line search — and, at Camp 12, why bigger tensors crawl: interfaces grow, \(L_k\) grows, steps \(1/L_k\) shrink.

Now operate PALM on the smallest honest specimen that already misbehaves: two unknown numbers \(a,b\), data "1", objective \(F(a,b)=\tfrac12(ab-1)^2+\lambda(|a|+|b|)\). It is a two-core tensor train in miniature — the product \(ab\) plays the role of \(\mathrm{TT}(G)\). The good valleys hug the hyperbola \(ab\approx1\). And the origin \((0,0)\) is a certified trap: near it, \(F\approx\tfrac12+\lambda(|a|+|b|)\ge F(0,0)\), so it is a genuine local minimum that reconstructs nothing. Click to start walkers; raise \(\lambda\) and watch the trap's catchment grow.

Camp 07 · stationarity

What “the walker stops” means

For a smooth function, "flat" means \(\nabla F(x)=0\). Our \(F\) has kinks, so we need the right generalization. The tool is the limiting subdifferential \(\partial F(x)\) from variational analysis (Rockafellar & Wets): a set of generalized slopes at \(x\). At smooth points it collapses to \(\{\nabla F(x)\}\); at the kink of \(|u|\) at zero it is the interval \([-1,1]\) — every line you can wedge under the V-shape.

Definition critical point (generalized Fermat rule)

\(x^\star\) is a critical point of \(F\) if \(0 \in \partial F(x^\star)\). For our composite \(F=f+g\) this reads: \(-\nabla f(x^\star) \in \partial g(x^\star)\) — the smooth downhill pull is exactly cancelled by some legal slope of the tax. Every local minimizer is critical; not every critical point is a minimizer.

The second pillar of the convergence proof is that PALM never wastes a sweep:

Lemma sufficient decrease (BST 2014, Lemma 3)

With stepsizes \(\tau_k = 1/(\gamma L_k)\), \(\gamma>1\), each PALM sweep satisfies \[ F(x^{t+1}) \;\le\; F(x^{t}) \;-\; \frac{\gamma-1}{2}\,\sum_k L_k\,\|x_k^{t+1}-x_k^{t}\|^2 . \] The objective drops by at least a constant times the squared movement. So if the walker moved, altitude was genuinely lost; summing over time, total squared movement is finite, hence step sizes \(\to 0\).

Steps shrinking to zero is necessary but not yet convergence — the walker could in principle drift forever along an ever-flatter contour, like a marble spiraling in a nearly-level basin without settling. Ruling that out requires the deepest tool in the kit, and it deserves its own camp.

Camp 08 · the engine of the proof

The Kurdyka–Łojasiewicz inequality, and the big theorem

The KL property is a promise about terrain quality: near any critical point, flatness in slope cannot outrun flatness in altitude. Formally:

Definition KL property with exponent \(\theta\)

\(F\) satisfies KL at a critical point \(x^\star\) if there exist \(c>0\), \(\theta\in[\tfrac12,1)\), and a neighborhood where \[ \bigl(F(x)-F(x^\star)\bigr)^{\theta} \;\le\; c\,\cdot\operatorname{dist}\!\bigl(0,\,\partial F(x)\bigr). \] Equivalently: after bending altitude by the desingularizing function \(\varphi(s)=\tfrac{c}{1-\theta}s^{1-\theta}\), the terrain has slope at least 1 — no infinitely lazy approaches allowed. The exponent \(\theta\) grades the laziness: \(\theta=\tfrac12\) is honest curvature; \(\theta\to1\) is pancake country.

Why is our \(F\) entitled to this promise? Because of a beautiful structural theorem: every semi-algebraic function — anything built from finitely many polynomial equalities/inequalities — satisfies KL at every point (Łojasiewicz 1963 for analytic; Bolte–Daniilidis–Lewis 2007 for the nonsmooth case). And \(F\) qualifies: the fit term is a polynomial in the core entries (products and sums only), \(|u|\) is semi-algebraic since its graph is \(\{(u,y): y\ge u,\ y\ge -u,\ (y-u)(y+u)=0\}\)-definable, and sums of semi-algebraic functions are semi-algebraic. No probabilistic assumptions, no convexity — pure algebra buys the property.

Theorem global convergence of PALM — Bolte, Sabach & Teboulle 2014

Let \(F=f+g\) be the composite above (each \(g_k\) proper lower-semicontinuous, \(f\) smooth with block-Lipschitz gradients) and semi-algebraic, and let \((x^t)\) be a bounded PALM sequence. Then the trajectory has finite length, \(\sum_t \|x^{t+1}-x^t\| < \infty\), and \(x^t\) converges to a single critical point \(x^\star\) of \(F\).

And the rate dictionary (Attouch–Bolte–Svaiter 2013; BST Thm. 2), keyed entirely by the exponent at the limit:

θ ∈ (1/2, 1)pancake / shelfpolynomial: error ~ t−(1−θ)/(2θ−1)

This race is finding number 3 of our study, in miniature. The theorem guarantees convergence for every exponent — but in our hardest cells (rank 4 × density 0.60), 75%±15 of runs at size \(6^3\) and up to 100% at \(10^3\) were still measurably moving after 8,000 sweeps, with step decay matching the pancake regime, while benign cells fit the straight line with \(R^2 > 0.99\). The KL exponent of sparse TT problems is not a constant of nature; it transitions across the phase diagram. Nobody has a theory of that transition. Yet.

Camp 09 · second-order vision

Kinds of flat: the curvature certificate

The slope (first derivative) is zero at every critical point — that is the definition. To tell flat spots apart you must poke the ground and feel the curvature: the second derivative. In many dimensions, curvature at a point is a matrix — the Hessian \(\nabla^2 f(x^\star)\), whose entry \((i,j)\) is \(\partial^2 f/\partial x_i \partial x_j\). Its eigenvalues are the curvatures along its natural axes, and they are the verdict:

Definition classification at a critical point

Let \(\lambda_{\min}\) be the smallest Hessian eigenvalue at a critical point. If \(\lambda_{\min}>0\): a local minimum (every direction climbs). If \(\lambda_{\min}<0\): a strict saddle (at least one strictly downhill escape direction exists). If \(\lambda_{\min}=0\): degenerate — a flat direction; the second derivative refuses to rule.

Experiment 7 — the curvature dial\(q(x,y)=\frac12(\lambda_1 x^2+\lambda_2 y^2)\)

One technical lock had to be picked before this dial could be used on our \(F\): the tax \(\lambda\|G\|_1\) has no second derivative at zero. The key observation: at a limit point, look only at the active support \(S\) — the coordinates that survived thresholding (nonzeros). On those coordinates, near the point, \(|u|\) is just \(\pm u\): locally linear, with second derivative exactly zero. So restricted to \(S\), all curvature comes from the smooth fit \(f\), and the classification is legitimate:

Instrument the restricted-Hessian certificate (ours)

At a converged PALM limit, form \(H_S = \bigl[\nabla^2 f(x^\star)\bigr]_{S\times S}\) — the Hessian of the fit term, rows and columns restricted to the surviving coordinates. We evaluate it without symbolic differentiation, by finite-difference Hessian–vector products \(H v \approx \frac{\nabla f(x+\varepsilon v)-\nabla f(x)}{\varepsilon}\), then take the full spectrum (dense, for \(|S|\le 600\)) or the extreme eigenvalues (Lanczos iteration, beyond). \(\lambda_{\min}(H_S) > \epsilon\): certified local minimum on its support. \(\lambda_{\min}(H_S) < -\epsilon\): certified strict saddle. Near-zero eigenvalues get counted against a budget — the gauge dimension of the next camp.

This certificate is what let us say, with numbers attached, sentences like “3 of 12 walkers converged to a genuine wrong valley — not a saddle they failed to escape.” Without it, a multi-start census only shows different altitudes; with it, every altitude gets a diagnosis.

Camp 10 · symmetry

Disguises: the gauge group, and the fact we proved

Here is a trap for the unwary surveyor. Just as \(6 = 2\times3 = 1\times6 = 4\times1.5\), a tensor-train has many core settings that rebuild the identical cube. Insert any invertible matrix \(M\) and its inverse at a bond:

\[ G_k \mapsto G_k M, \qquad G_{k+1} \mapsto M^{-1} G_{k+1} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad G_k M\, M^{-1} G_{k+1} = G_k G_{k+1}. \]

This relabeling freedom is called gauge, and it has dimension \(\sum_k r_k^2\) (one full matrix per interior bond). Consequences are everywhere: “different” limit points may be the same answer in costume (so we measure recovery on the rebuilt cube, which strips all costumes); and every gauge direction is a perfectly flat direction of the fit term — free zero eigenvalues that the curvature certificate must expect and excuse.

Now the subtle question that produced our one new theorem. Sparsity seems like it should destroy gauge: a generic matrix \(M\) smears zeros into nonzeros, leaving the sparse set. True — for generic \(M\). But one subgroup survives any sparsity pattern:

Proposition 1 diagonal gauge survives sparsity — proved in our draft

Let \(D=\mathrm{diag}(d_1,\dots,d_{r_k})\) with all \(d_i\neq 0\). Then \(G_k \mapsto G_k D,\; G_{k+1}\mapsto D^{-1}G_{k+1}\) (i) leaves the rebuilt tensor unchanged, and (ii) leaves the support — the set of nonzero entries — of every core unchanged. Differentiating the curves \(D(t)=\mathrm{diag}(e^{t a_1},\dots,e^{t a_{r_k}})\) at \(t=0\) yields, at any cores, a subspace of dimension \(\sum_{k=1}^{d-1} r_k\) of support-preserving directions in the kernel of the Jacobian of the TT map. Hence at any exact-fit point, the restricted Hessian \(H_S\) has at least \(\sum_k r_k\) zero eigenvalues — sparsity or no sparsity.

Proof

(i) is the bond-insertion identity above with \(M=D\). (ii): right-multiplying by \(D\) rescales the \(i\)-th bond-column of \(G_k\) by \(d_i\neq0\), and left-multiplying by \(D^{-1}\) rescales the \(i\)-th bond-row of \(G_{k+1}\) by \(1/d_i\) — rescaling by a nonzero number maps zeros to zeros and nonzeros to nonzeros, so supports are fixed. For the kernel statement: the curve \(t\mapsto (G_k D(t), D(t)^{-1}G_{k+1})\) stays inside both the support set and the fiber of constant rebuilt tensor; its velocity at \(t=0\) is therefore a support-tangent vector annihilated by the TT Jacobian \(J\). At a zero-residual point, \(\nabla^2 f = J^{\top}J\), which kills these directions. The \(a\)-parameters across bonds give \(\sum_k r_k\) independent ones generically. ∎

Operate the disguise machine: one bond channel of a two-core toy, slider \(t\) applying \(e^{t}\) to the left core and \(e^{-t}\) to the right. Watch three meters — the rebuilt value (frozen), the support (frozen), and the \(\ell_1\) tax (moving!):

The third meter is the punchline twice over. First, since the tax changes along a fit-flat direction, the full objective \(F\) is not flat there — the tax weakly pins the disguise, which is why our converged points still classified cleanly. Second, it explains a failure we measured: “fixing” the gauge by QR-orthogonalizing cores (a standard numerical trick) drags solutions through generic gauge transforms, and our gauge-fixed PALM densified cores from ~10–30% to ~45–50% nonzeros. The tax and the orthogonal gauge are enemies; only the diagonal gauge is sparsity's friend — which became our Conjecture 3.

Camp 11 · methodology

The survey protocol, instrument by instrument

With the theory assembled, here is the exact pipeline we ran — every run, every number in the next camp came from this loop:

Manufacture a truth

Draw sparse TT cores: Gaussian entries × Bernoulli(ρ) zero-mask, ranks (1,r,r,1), shape 6³. Rebuild the cube, add 1% Gaussian noise. Reject degenerate all-zero draws. We know the answer — that is the point.

Drop 12 walkers

12 random initializations of PALM, each given 8,000 sweeps, exact block Lipschitz steps (Camp 06), γ = 1.1, λ = 0.1 × RMS(𝒯).

Declare convergence honestly

A run “converged” only if its last sweep moved less than 10⁻⁵·max(1,‖𝒯‖). Non-converged runs are reported as such — they are finding 3, not noise.

Cluster the stopping altitudes

Group final objective values within relative tolerance 5×10⁻³. Each cluster = one candidate critical point (modulo disguise).

Certify each cluster

Restricted-Hessian spectrum (Camp 09) on a converged representative: minimum on its support / strict saddle / degenerate, with the diagonal-gauge zero-eigenvalue budget (Camp 10) excused.

Score recovery, disguise-proof

‖TT(Ĝ) − 𝒯‖_F / ‖𝒯‖_F on rebuilt cubes — gauge-invariant by construction. A “spurious minimum” = certified minimum with ≥ 2× the best cluster's error.

Polish and compare

Debias the best basin (freeze support, λ=0, projected descent). Run one TT-SVD-initialized walker to test the smart-start hypothesis. Fit step-decay curves for the empirical KL exponent.

Replicate, then scale

5 independent truths per (r, ρ) cell × 9 cells = 45 censuses, ~540 walks; plus 6 censuses at 10³. Every frequency below is a mean ± sd over truths, not an anecdote.

The four findings, stated plainly

1 — A benign regime exists. Thin chains are friendly terrain: at rank 2–3 with low density, 77–95% of walkers reach one dominant basin, certificates read “minimum,” and step decay is the straight line of KL exponent ½. This is where a future benign-landscape theorem should live.

2 — Trap valleys are instance-dependent. Certified spurious minima appeared in 22% of truths — including particular rank-2 truths where 33–42% of walkers were captured, while sibling truths in the same cell had none. Pathology is decided by the fine structure of the specific true support, not by the coarse (r, ρ) knobs. Any honest theorem must be conditional on the support.

3 — At scale, the enemy is the exponent. The hard corner is not full of traps — it is full of pancake. Non-convergence within 8,000 sweeps reaches 75%±15 at 6³ and dominates at 10³, exactly the polynomial-crawl regime of the rate table at Camp 08. Among walkers that did settle, polished error stayed ≈ 0.01 — the good valleys persist; reaching them is what gets slow.

4 — Practical levers work, conditionally. TT-SVD smart starts found the best basin in 78% of truths (failures concentrated where basins fragment). Debiasing improved error in 26/28 comparisons, median 31%. Inertia (iPALM, β=0.5) removed saddle-stagnation in spot checks. Orthogonal gauge-fixing backfired exactly as Proposition 1 predicts.

Camp 13 · honesty & horizon

Dead ends, and the trails we marked

Two pre-registered guesses died, and we kept the bodies. Guess A: truths whose interface matrices are ill-conditioned (κ = max over bonds of cond(L)·cond(R)) breed traps. Guess B: truths with a thin identifiability margin μ — the first restricted-Hessian eigenvalue past the diagonal-gauge budget — breed traps. Both tested against all 45 truths: Spearman correlations |ρ| ≤ 0.16, p ≥ 0.28. Null and null. A clean negative is a gift to theorists: the two analyses anyone would attempt first are now known not to be the mechanism. Whatever decides which truths carry traps is not local geometry at the optimum — our money is on global basin structure, or the ratio of λ to the smallest true nonzero (entries below the guillotine effectively change which problem is being solved).

Three conjectures, marked on the map for whoever climbs next. C1: under combinatorial conditions on the true supports (excluding the 22% degenerate class), every certified minimum with matching support is global modulo permutation + diagonal scaling, and all else is strict saddle. C2: in that regime the KL exponent is exactly ½ (linear PALM), and outside it instances force exponents in (½,1) — the observed crawl. C3, the cheapest to test: replace QR gauge-fixing with diagonal-only bond rescaling — support-safe by Proposition 1, prox-compatible, bounded block constants — and the slow-KL regime attributable to scale drift disappears without densification.

Limitations, on the record. Order d=3 only; one base size with a three-cell check at 10³; 5 truths × 12 walkers per cell; one λ rule; finite-difference Hessians; loss-value clustering could merge distinct equal-altitude critical points (none observed, none excluded); the equal-budget size comparison partially conflates landscape with conditioning. The scale-up script in the bundle erases most of these.
